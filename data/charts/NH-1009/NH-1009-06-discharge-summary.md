# Discharge Summary — Gideon Ashworth (NH-1009)

## Admission and Discharge

- Admitted: 2024-08-09
- Discharged: 2024-08-12
- Admitting service: Hospital Medicine

## Principal Diagnosis

Cellulitis, left lower leg.

## Allergies

Myrexatin (tavoxine) — hives.

## Hospital Course

Mr. Ashworth presented with three days of spreading redness, warmth and tenderness of the left lower leg after a minor abrasion sustained while gardening. On arrival he had a temperature of 38.4 °C, heart rate 98 and a white blood cell count of 13.8 x10^3/µL. The area of erythema was outlined at admission. Ultrasound of the leg showed no abscess and no deep collection.

Because of the listed Myrexatin allergy (hives), the team avoided Myrexatin and treated him with Quinavort (selvoxin) IV, transitioned to Quinavort by mouth on hospital day 2 once fever resolved. The erythema receded well inside the marked borders by hospital day 3. White blood cell count normalized to 8.9 x10^3/µL before discharge. The leg was elevated and he ambulated independently throughout the stay.

## Discharge Condition

Afebrile, ambulating independently, erythema markedly improved.

## Discharge Medications

- Quinavort (selvoxin) by mouth to complete a total 10-day antibiotic course.
- Dermasolix (quazoderm) 0.1% cream, thin layer twice daily to forearm plaques (home medication, unchanged).

## Follow-up

- Primary care visit in 1 week to recheck the leg.
- Return sooner for fever, spreading redness or increasing pain.
- Consider Allergy and Immunology evaluation of the Myrexatin allergy label at a future outpatient visit.
